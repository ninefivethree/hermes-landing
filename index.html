Frequently Asked Questions

What is Hermes?

Hermes is an urban mobility service offering on-demand access to a vast network of innovative personal transportation solutions Whether you're commuting, exploring the city, or solving the last mile, Hermes provides the right vehicle for every journey.

How do I find a vehicle?

Open the Hermes app and tap "Find a Ride". Alternatively, look for our signature black and yellow livery and signage around the city.

Do I need a driver's license?

It depends on your city and type of vehicle. G-type, R-type, and most other non-living vehicles are available to any user with a valid Hermes account who has verified their age and completed our in-app safety training. Certain high-speed vehicles may require a valid driver's license for use on public roads, depending on local regulations. Check the Hermes app for specific requirements in your area.

Are Hermes vehicles street-legal?

We work closely with local authorities to ensure the vehicles we deploy in a given city are compliant with local traffic laws and regulations. With that being said, some vehicles are better suited for bike lanes or pedestrian paths. The Hermes app will let you know of any restrictions or requirements for your chosen vehicle in advance.

Are the horses okay?

Hermes maintains rigorous welfare standards across our entire living fleet in partnership with certified animal care providers. All E-type and EM-type units undergo daily health assessments and rotating rest periods. All E-type parking stations are equipped with ample food and water supplies, as well as climate-controlled shelters and a trained team of caretakers. The horses are doing very well.

When is Hermes coming to my country/city/province?

Our network is always expanding. Due to the complex and multifaceted nature of our business, we are unable to provide specific timelines for new city launches at this time. However, we do publish regular updates via our newsletter and social media channels.

Can I take a Hermes vehicle on public transit with me?

No.

Why is it called Hermes?

Hermes is the Greek god of travel, roads, and speed. We couldn't think of anything else.
